How it works

Read every answer. Act on the pattern. Keep the outlier.

A one-page dashboard that turns open-ended survey responses into themes a program team can act on the same week.

The problem

End-of-year surveys for a cross-district leadership network came back as long, open-ended answers. Read one by one, they blurred together.

The team needed three things: what worked, what people needed next, and which single voices deserved attention even though they weren't the majority.

From raw answers to a planning meeting

The work is in the coding. The page is how the finding travels.

Collect

Three open prompts

Strengths, needs, and open suggestions, answered in about ten minutes at the end of a session. The demo has 30 invented responses.

→
Code

Themes from raw text

Every answer gets read and coded into themes. Themes come out of what people actually wrote, not a list chosen in advance.

→
Count

Frequency, not volume

Strengths and needs chart by how many people raised each theme. Suggestions become short cards with a count.

→
Protect

Signal watch

Outlier and dissenting voices get their own section, so a small but important signal doesn't get averaged away.

Architecture

Deliberately small. A page that opens anywhere and needs nothing to run.

Front end

Single HTML file

No build step. The coded data lives in the page, so it opens offline and can be emailed as one file.

Charts

Chart.js

Horizontal bars sized for long theme labels, with wrapped tick labels so nothing gets cut off on a phone.

Layout

Phone-first

It reads on a phone as well as a laptop, so it can be shared straight into a planning meeting.

Privacy

No backend at all

Nothing to secure and nothing to leak. Only coded themes and selected quotes ever reach the page.

Design decisions

1

Themes, not word clouds

A word cloud shows which words were common. Coded themes show what people meant, and a team can act on that.

2

Tell it in the order a team decides

Strengths first, then needs, then suggestions, then outliers. That's the order a planning conversation actually runs.

3

A home for the dissenting view

Signal watch exists because the contrarian answer is often the early warning. Averages bury it.

4

Counts on every card

Every suggestion card shows how many people raised it, so one loud request doesn't read like a consensus.

5

Invented data in public

The real responses stay private. This build uses invented answers and says so in the header.

What it shows about the work

This is the same job a customer success lead does with support tickets and feedback forms: find the patterns, protect the outliers, and hand the product team something specific enough to build on.
